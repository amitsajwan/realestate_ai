# Kharadi, Upper Kharadi or Wagholi? How to choose in 10 minutes

Three neighbourhoods, one corridor, very different daily lives. If you are shortlisting a home on Pune's east side, this is the
quick way to decide where to spend your weekends viewing flats.

## The one-line version

- **Kharadi**: closest to the jobs. Established, busier, usually the most expensive of the three.
- **Upper Kharadi**: the middle path. Newer projects, more open space, a short hop to Kharadi's offices.
- **Wagholi**: more space for the money and newer towns, but a longer commute and more dependence on the road you take.

*(We do not quote prices per sq ft here. They differ by project, tower, floor and year, and they change. Ask the seller for the full cost sheet and compare on carpet area.)*

## What actually decides it

### 1. Your commute (be honest about it)
Kharadi is home to large office campuses such as EON IT Park and World Trade Center Pune, so staying nearby can mean a short trip.
Wagholi is further out. **Do the real test:** drive or ride from the flat to your office at 9:00 am and 6:30 pm on a weekday, not on a Sunday.

### 2. Metro: approved is not the same as running
The Union Cabinet has approved Pune Metro Phase-2, including **Corridor 2B, Ramwadi to Wagholi/Vitthalwadi (about 11.6 km, 11 stations)**,
and **Line 4, Kharadi to Khadakwasla**. That is good news for this corridor. But an approved line takes years to build.
**Do not pay extra today for a metro that is not yet running.** Check the current status on the official Maha-Metro website before you decide.
Sources: [PIB: Corridor 2B (Ramwadi to Wagholi)](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2139488&reg=48&lang=2) ·
[PIB: Line 4 (Kharadi to Khadakwasla)](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/cabinet-approves-pune-metro-rail-project-phase-2-kharadi-khadakwasla-line-4-nal-stop-warje-manik-baug-line-4a/)

### 3. Ready-to-move or under construction
Wagholi and Upper Kharadi have many projects still being built. Under construction can mean a lower entry price, but you wait, and you rely on
the builder keeping the promised date. Ready-to-move means you see exactly what you buy. Decide which risk you prefer.

### 4. The everyday basics
Visit at rush hour and after rain. Ask about **water supply** (tanker or municipal or borewell), **power backup**, and **parking**.
Look at the road in front of the project, not just the brochure.

## The 5-point checklist before you book anything

1. **RERA registration** of the project, and its stated possession date, on the MahaRERA website.
2. **Carpet area** in the agreement, and the price per sq ft of carpet area (so you can compare fairly).
3. **Title and approvals**: get a lawyer to check them.
4. **Possession date and the builder's earlier projects**: visit one that is finished.
5. **Full cost**: stamp duty, registration, GST where applicable, parking, maintenance deposit.

## So which one?

- Choose **Kharadi** if a short commute matters most and your budget is comfortable.
- Choose **Upper Kharadi** if you want newer towers and more open space without going as far out as Wagholi.
- Choose **Wagholi** if you want more space for your budget and can live with the commute (and the wait for the metro).
